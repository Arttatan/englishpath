The present simple of be: Form

Welcome! The verb be (am / is / are) is one of the first verbs you need in English. You use it almost every day — to say who you are, how you feel, where something is, and much more.

In English, be has three present forms. Learn them well — they appear in almost every conversation.

Remember: I → am  ·  he / she / it → is  ·  we / you / they → are

The present simple of be: Form

Positive I am / I’m You are / You’re He is / He’s She is / She’s It is / It’s We are / We’re They are / They’re
Negative I am not / I’m not You are not / You’re not / You aren’t He is not / He’s not / He isn’t She is not / She’s not / She isn’t It is not / It’s not / It isn’t We are not / We’re not / We aren’t They are not / They’re not / They aren’t
Question Am I …? Are you …? Is he …? Is she …? Is it …? Are we …? Are they …?

Short answers — Yes

Yes, I am Yes, you are Yes, he is Yes, she is Yes, it is Yes, we are Yes, they are

Short answers — No

No, I’m not No, you aren’t / you’re not No, he isn’t / he’s not No, she isn’t / she’s not No, it isn’t / it’s not No, we aren’t / we’re not No, they aren’t / they’re not
Important rule: We cannot use short forms in positive short answers.
A: Are you happy? B: Yes, I’m.
A: Are you happy? B: Yes, I am.

The three forms (say them aloud)

  • I am (I’m)
  • He / She / It is (he’s / she’s / it’s)
  • We / You / They are (we’re / you’re / they’re)

They = people and things (more than one).

He, she, it, they — who or what?

  • We use he for a man or a boy.
  • We use she for a woman or a girl.
  • We use it for a thing, an animal (sometimes), the weather, time, and “this/that”.
  • We use they for people and for things (plural).
He’s a little boy.
She’s beautiful.
I like this TV. It’s very big.
I love Sara and Jonas. They are my friends.
I love these chairs. They are very beautiful.

Don’t forget the subject

In English we almost always need a subject (I, you, she, the weather, Tom…) before the verb. Beginners often forget this — please don’t!

It is cold.   Is cold.
Sally is a wonderful woman.   Is a wonderful woman.

Contracted (short) forms

We often use short forms with personal pronouns: ’m, ’s, ’re.

  • I’m sad.
  • You’re tired.
  • She’s from Scotland.

We can also use ’s with names and places:

  • Laura’s beautiful.
  • London’s an expensive city.

In everyday American English, short forms are very common when we speak and in messages to friends.

Contractions in short answers

We can use contractions in negative short answers. We do not use them in positive short answers.

  • Yes, I am.   Yes, I’m.
  • Yes, she is.   Yes, she’s.
  • Yes, they are.   Yes, they’re.
  • No, I’m not. / No, she isn’t.

The present simple of be — USE

We use be to talk about many everyday things. Look at the picture chart, then read the examples slowly.

Different uses of be

👋

Introducing people

I’m Steven, and this is Isabella. And this is my daughter, Alicia.

🎂

Age

I’m 24 years old, and my father is 50.

💼

Job

I’m a teacher, and my wife is a doctor.

🏳️

Nationality / origin

Alex is from Ireland, and Sonia is from Spain.

💭

Feelings

I’m scared. She is very tired.

🎨

Colour

Our dog is black. His eyes are blue.

💲

Prices

It’s seven dollars. This T-shirt is twenty dollars.

☀️

Weather

It’s sunny today. It is very cold this morning.

⏰

Time

What time is it? It’s ten past four.

🔍

Descriptions

He is very short and thin. These cars are old and cheap.

Study tip for absolute beginners

  1. First learn: I am / he is / they are.
  2. Then practice questions: Am I…? Is she…? Are they…?
  3. Then practice short answers: Yes, I am. / No, I’m not.
  4. Finally, notice when we use be (age, job, weather…).

When you finish reading, go back to Exercises and try Exercise 1 again. Mistakes are normal — that is how you learn!
